# sync v1: triggers tell, the listener tells the readers, answers say what they read

> Builds on [sync keeps screens in step with the database](../../2026-10-22-sync-keeps-screens-in-step.md), [Postgres rings the bell](../../2026-10-12-postgres-rings-the-bell.md), [Who is rung comes from who may see](../../2026-10-13-who-is-rung-comes-from-who-may-see.md) and [Asking again, and bells that miss nothing](../../2026-10-14-asking-again-and-bells-that-miss-nothing.md).

**Decision:**

- **Every table map says `:sync`:** `:by-access` (the people its `:access` sentences let read the row), `:everyone`, or `[:silent "why"]`. A table that says nothing, `:by-access` without `:access`, or `:silent` without a reason stops the start, every problem at once. Groundwork's own tables are silent, with their reasons.
- **The trigger:** a table that tells gets three statement triggers (insert, update, delete; made by groundwork.server.database from `:on-change`) calling one function, `groundwork.tell_sync`. It sends, when the transaction commits, `{folder, table, ids, columns}`: the changed rows' ids and the distinct values of the columns the readers name (`order__shop_id`), before and after. A statement that changed no row sends nothing; past 7500 characters only the table is sent (`"all": true`).
- **Who is told:** every server listens (one held connection) and gathers what arrives within 50 ms. Each change goes to the open sse lines whose person may read one of the rows: `:anyone` every line, `:signed-in` every line with a person, `[:me col]` the persons the column names, `[:member col]` the people holding a reading action in one of the scopes the column names (one query per reader for the batch, Owners included). A sentence's `:when` is left out: more told, never fewer.
- **The message** is `{:sync/changed {:table "orders" :ids ["…"]}}`. After a break in listening every line hears `{:sync/missed {}}`.
- **Answers say what they read:** around a handler's own steps (not the session or the access lookup), every query's tables are noted, from Postgres's planner (once per query), and sent beside the answer as transport notes: `{:groundwork/notes {:orders/all {:tables ["orders"]}}}`; `"any"` when Postgres can't plan one of them. A function that reads tables inside hides them; its query names them (`-- reads: orders`).

**Why:** what a screen shows and who may see it are both already known (the queries, the sentences); nothing new to declare and forget. Writes from anywhere (a handler, a background job, a hand-run fix) are told, and a rolled-back transaction tells nothing.

**The price, and what v1 leaves out:**
- A line's person is found when it opens; signing out elsewhere doesn't close it, so until it reopens it can still hear ids (never data) of changes.
- `:when` is ignored, so a reader may hear of a row it can't see (its id only).
- The writer's own answer doesn't yet carry the change ("a write's answer carries its own bell", 2026-10-14); the writer hears it over sse like everyone else.
- Access tables are silent: a changed membership is seen on the next message, not pushed.

**Instead of:** a handler listing the tables it reads (a forgotten one leaves a screen stale with no error); telling every line of every change (reads grow with the square of the scopes online); the database's change stream (a replication slot that fills the disk if nobody reads it).
